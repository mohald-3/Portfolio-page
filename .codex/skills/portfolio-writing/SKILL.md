# Portfolio Writing

Improve portfolio copy without changing its facts, functionality, or intended meaning. Write as Mohanad explaining his work to developers, engineering managers, technical recruiters, and product or technology leads.

## Voice

Use a calm, analytical, practical, technically curious, concrete, and understated voice. Keep it professional without sounding corporate, academic, promotional, or artificially casual.

- Prefer direct, natural language and simple wording.
- Show qualities through decisions and examples instead of personality adjectives.
- Explain what was built, why it mattered, what constraints existed, and how technical problems were handled.
- When useful, let the explanation follow problem, consideration, implementation, and result. Do not force this pattern into every paragraph.
- Vary sentence length and openings naturally. Avoid slogan-like fragments and repetitive paragraph structures.
- Preserve the page's existing language. Use clear international English or natural modern Swedish with a direct, understated tone.

Avoid generic claims and AI-style language such as "passionate about," "thrive on," "cutting-edge," "innovative solutions," "leveraging," "harnessing the power of," "seamless," "game-changing," "results-driven," "highly motivated," "journey," "empower," and "pushing boundaries." Do not replace one cliche with another.

Never use an em dash (U+2014). Use a comma, colon, full stop, parentheses, or a rewritten sentence. Normal hyphens remain appropriate in terms such as "full-stack" and "role-based."

## Technical credibility and accuracy

- Keep useful technologies, numbers, architecture decisions, responsibilities, and implementation details.
- Connect technologies to the work they enabled instead of presenting technology dumps.
- Prefer evidence over adjectives. Relevant supported facts may include a REST API with 60+ endpoints, 300+ automated tests, synchronized data covering 197 Swedish cities, production work across backend, web, and mobile, and apps published to both major app stores. Use a detail only where the source supports it and it improves the passage.
- Never invent or exaggerate technologies, employment, experience, ownership, metrics, impact, decisions, or outcomes.
- Distinguish professional work, project work, education, experiments, and technologies being learned.
- Flag questionable source claims instead of polishing them into stronger claims.
- Use direct ownership verbs such as "built," "developed," "implemented," or "maintained" when supported. Do not imply sole ownership when the evidence does not support it.

Mohanad has a Civil Engineering degree in Architectural Engineering and House Building. Mention it only when it meaningfully explains systems thinking, analytical problem solving, complex systems, or his move into software.

When AI-assisted development is relevant, describe practical use in analysis, implementation, debugging, testing, or review, while making clear that Mohanad reviews the work and owns the final solution. Do not make AI central to his developer identity.

## Content priorities

For project descriptions, prioritize:

1. What the product does.
2. Mohanad's responsibility.
3. The interesting technical problem.
4. How it was solved.
5. Relevant technology.
6. The result or current state.

Do not force every description into all six points or open every project with the same phrase.

For About copy, present Mohanad as a full-stack developer with a backend focus who considers whole systems, unclear problems, failure paths, maintainability, testing, and deployment. Demonstrate these traits through examples rather than listing adjectives, and do not make the section read like a cover letter.

## Editing workflow

When editing the codebase:

1. Inspect the requested files and nearby user-visible copy before editing.
2. Establish factual support from the existing portfolio and user-provided context. Ask only when a missing fact would materially change the result.
3. Rewrite only text that is genuinely improved. Preserve good natural copy.
4. Preserve components, application logic, routes, variables, API fields, links, and technical identifiers unless the user explicitly requests related changes.
5. Review the complete affected page for repeated wording, inconsistent voice, unsupported claims, and language consistency.
6. Search the edited files for U+2014 and remove every occurrence introduced or retained in user-visible portfolio copy.
7. Run proportionate existing checks when source files changed, and report any factual uncertainty separately.

Before finishing, confirm that the copy sounds like a developer speaking naturally, explains real work rather than advertising Mohanad, retains useful specifics, contains no unsupported claim, avoids inflated wording, and contains no em dash.
